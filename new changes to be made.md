  <h2>The Gendered Staffing Gap: Female Leadership and the Formation of Startup Workforce</h2>

  <details class="paper-abstract">
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    <p>Recent research on gender inequality and entrepreneurship shows that female-led startups face disadvantages in attracting job applicants. Yet whether a venture attracts applicants and whether it staffs particular occupational roles are distinct questions. I distinguish hiring and staffing as complementary but analytically distinct phenomena and use administrative employment records covering 21,330 Brazilian technology startups to examine whether gender disparities extend to the occupational roles ventures ultimately staff.</p>

    <p>To explain this pattern, I develop an evaluation-based account of when worker-level hiring disadvantages translate into a staffing deficit for female-led startups. Consistent with this account, the deficit is larger in male-typed occupations, where founder gender is more salient, and for roles the venture has not previously staffed, where workers have less role-specific information. The deficit narrows with the increased availability of prospective workers in the local labor market, leaving workers with fewer employment alternatives and less scope to act on unfavorable evaluations.</p>

    <p>Together, these findings show that gender differences in startup hiring extend beyond whether female-led ventures attract workers to shape which occupational roles they are ultimately able to staff.</p>
